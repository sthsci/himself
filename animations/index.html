Gold under two microscopes

SEM: why gold looks bright

In scanning electron microscopy (SEM), a beam scans gold particles on carbon. More electrons bounce back from gold, so the detector records brighter pixels there.

TEM: why gold looks dark

In bright-field transmission electron microscopy (TEM), the beam passes through a thin film. Gold scatters electrons outside the aperture, so fewer reach the camera and the particles look dark.

Predator–prey cycles

The Lotka–Volterra model

Each birth, hunt or predator death moves the dot on the population plot. Prey numbers rise first. Predators follow, prey become scarce, and predator numbers fall until the prey can recover.
